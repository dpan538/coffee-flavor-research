# Duplicate and publication-layer audit

## Count surfaces

Round 3M never collapses the following into one number:

1. segmented atomic observations;
2. assertion-level observations after within-record exact-repeat removal;
3. effective-record-level unique descriptor observations.

The descriptor census reports 305, 303, and 302 respectively. These values are
retained as report receipt only because its machine-readable rows are absent.
The live pilot publishes its own independently reproducible counts and does not
force them to equal the report.

For the live pilot, 140 segmented assertions become 139 assertion-level rows
after one exact within-field repeat loss, then 137 record-level unique rows
after two cross-observation same-coffee repeat losses. Its three surfaces are
therefore `140 / 139 / 137`; the coincidental proximity to the report's
`305 / 303 / 302` does not establish report-row reproduction.

## Distinct repeat relationships

The schema distinguishes exact within-field repetition, within-record
repetition, cross-observation same-coffee repetition, another competition
round, another preparation service, mirror publication, summary/detail
publication layers, and true duplicate source artifacts.

The same coffee in a genuine new round or service may remain a distinct
effective record while retaining identity linkage. A mirror never creates an
independent family. Primary jury fields, generic organizer fields, producer
profiles, and secondary tables are not merged merely because they appear on
one page.

The contract can structurally retain a distinct secondary publication row with
its own artifact, schema, locator, and observation identity, but it assigns a
non-counting disposition. A transaction-local SQL fixture proves this path and
is rolled back; the current live pilot has zero secondary review-only rows.
This preserves future evidence for review without allowing a secondary layer
to enter de-inflated or co-assertion counts or demote primary credit.

The 376 AVPA rows are rejected as non-descriptors rather than counted as a
duplicate loss; their error is semantic class, not publication duplication.
