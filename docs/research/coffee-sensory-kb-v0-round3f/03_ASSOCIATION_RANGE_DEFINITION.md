# Association range definition

An association range is a bounded, overlapping research grouping of
expressions, concepts or references that are frequently, relatively or
contextually associated under named evidence.

The initial anchors are exactly the seven regions already used by governed
question-language research:

- floral / tea;
- fruit;
- cocoa / nut / caramel;
- roast / spice / smoke;
- sweet-associated character;
- acidity character;
- texture / body / drying.

They are not universal dimensions, mutually exclusive flavor families,
ontology categories, equal-distance clusters, latent biological truths or final
user-visible sections.

A subject may have zero, one or multiple memberships. No `OTHER` range exists.
Membership roles are limited to `ANCHOR`, `FREQUENT_ASSOCIATE`,
`CONTEXTUAL_ASSOCIATE`, `PERIPHERAL_CANDIDATE` and `AMBIGUOUS`. A role is not a
probability.

Every membership records an evidence basis, evidence key and provenance path.
Without admissible evidence the disposition is `UNRESOLVED`, `AMBIGUOUS`,
`SOURCE_LOCAL_ONLY` or `OUTSIDE_CURRENT_RANGE_MODEL`.

Range co-membership does not imply synonymy, hierarchy, sensory-neighbour
status, fixed distance, ingredient identity, causality, objective probability,
cross-cultural equivalence or transitivity. Absence does not imply contrast.
