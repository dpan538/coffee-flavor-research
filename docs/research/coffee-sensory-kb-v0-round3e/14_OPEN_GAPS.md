# Open gaps

## Empirical gaps

- No real Round 3C/3D pilot observation exists.
- No imported source crosses the required C0 × seven-level C1 matrix with
  sensory outcomes and independent replicates.
- Milk preparation appears in lexical/context evidence, but imported controlled
  sensory outcomes contain zero declared milk-condition observations.
- No imported Simplified-Chinese ordinary-user sensory outcome table exists.
- Preparation and roast interactions, question information gain, candidate
  ranking, context priors, stopping rules and adaptive policy remain
  `NOT_ESTIMABLE`.

## Evidence acquisition gaps

- Two data-on-request sources require human-authorized correspondence; no
  request was sent.
- The Round 2B tasting-note corpus remains commercially source-concentrated.
  Round 3E found no second lawful, immutable, redistributable contemporary
  specialty tasting-note corpus suitable for raw import.
- The FT-NIR score snapshot lacks preparation and assessor protocol fields.
- The taste-sensitivity dataset lacks sufficient coffee-condition/codebook
  detail for pooling or context estimation.
- Measured roast colour does not yet connect source-specific language to the
  project’s seven interaction categories.

## Review and operational gaps

- New Chinese wording needs independent Mainland sensory/register review.
- All new questions need ordinary-user comprehension testing after approval.
- Ethics/governance approval, named investigators, complaints route,
  compensation amounts, retention periods and public-release license remain
  unresolved.
- Facility availability, roast/color measurement method, ingredient quantities,
  water system, staffing, insurance, total budget and procurement lead times
  require quotes and non-participant rehearsal.

Recommended next phase: obtain governance and bilingual review, send only
authorized data-access requests, close operational specifications and run a
non-participant preparation-throughput rehearsal. Human collection remains
closed until every gate is approved.
