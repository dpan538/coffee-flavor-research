# Interview and usability guide

## Status and use

`PROTOCOL_STATUS=PLANNED`

This is a reusable guide, not evidence that sessions occurred. Activation
requires a reviewed consent notice, recruitment plan, privacy/retention setup,
facilitator procedure, and separately authorized fieldwork.

## Intended session structure

### 1. Welcome and consent

- Explain the research purpose and voluntary nature.
- State that the activity is not a tasting exam.
- Explain recording, note-taking, optional free text, retention, withdrawal,
  and any later model-use request separately.
- Confirm consent version before collecting research data.

### 2. Background interview

- Tell me about the last time two coffees tasted different to you.
- What words did you use, if any?
- How do you respond to tasting notes on a bag, menu, or website?
- Which coffee terms feel familiar, useful, confusing, or intimidating?
- How do you remember a coffee you liked?

Avoid naming candidate descriptors before the participant has described their
own language.

### 3. Baseline description task

Ask the participant to describe a current or recalled coffee in their own
words. Record whether they use broad, specific, hedged, affective, preparation,
roast, or package-derived language. Do not score the response as correct or
incorrect.

### 4. Prototype task

Prompt: “Use this interface to find words that might help you compare and
remember your impression. You may reject every suggestion.”

Observe:

- interpretation of C0 and C1;
- hesitation, question wording, and answer strategy;
- whether context appears to anchor later responses;
- understanding of primary versus secondary candidates;
- use of none-of-these or abstention;
- navigation, keyboard/mobile access, and completion burden; and
- reactions to evidence and uncertainty explanations.

The current app does not implement the full adaptive flow. A prototype study
must identify which surfaces are live and which are moderated or conceptual.

### 5. Post-task interview

- Which reference, if any, felt useful? Why?
- Did any question or candidate push you toward an answer?
- What did “primary” and “secondary” mean to you?
- How would you describe the coffee now?
- How would you compare the result with package notes?
- When would you use or avoid this product?
- What would make you trust it more or less?

### 6. Recall probe

If the protocol includes a delayed task, ask the participant to recall or use
the selected descriptor without revealing the previous answer. The delay must
be specified in the activated protocol; it may not be invented after collection.

## Facilitator safeguards

- Do not praise one sensory answer as more accurate.
- Do not disclose package notes before the unbiased description task unless that
  comparison is the experimental condition.
- Ask neutral follow-ups and record contradictions.
- Do not collect unnecessary identifiers.
- Do not paste private free text into public issues, logs, or Git.
- Stop collection on withdrawal and follow the deletion workflow.

## Outputs

An activated session should produce a pseudonymous session receipt, consent
version, protocol version, observation notes with bounded access, a data-quality
status, and separately coded insight records. Quotes require explicit quotation
consent and must never be fabricated.
