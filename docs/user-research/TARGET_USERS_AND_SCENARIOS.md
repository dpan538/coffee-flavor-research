# Target users and scenarios

## Primary target users

### Curious café customer

Notices a difference among menu options and wants accessible words to compare
them without completing a professional form.

### Home brewer

Changes beans, grind, water, or preparation and wants to record impressions in
a more repeatable vocabulary.

### Beginner specialty-coffee drinker

Encounters professional or package descriptors but is unsure how literal,
specific, or transferable those terms are.

### Person comparing perception with a bean or menu card

Wants to understand agreement or disagreement without assuming the printed note
is an objective answer key.

### Person with broad perception but limited descriptor vocabulary

Can say “bright,” “strong,” “fruit-like,” or “dry” but may not know which more
specific reference is helpful.

Professional cuppers are not the primary audience. They may later contribute
qualification-scoped review or method critique under a separate evidence
contract.

## Core scenarios

| Scenario                | User goal                              | Product support                                 | Research risk                       |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Café comparison         | Name a contrast between two cups       | brief context and reference candidates          | time pressure and suggestion bias   |
| Home-brew journal       | Remember a recurring impression        | stable descriptor identity and comparison       | false precision across brew changes |
| Package-note comparison | Interpret or question a printed note   | candidate definitions and explicit disagreement | treating marketing text as truth    |
| Vocabulary learning     | Move from broad to more specific words | primary/secondary candidates and related terms  | cognitive overload                  |
| No match                | Reject unsuitable references           | visible abstention / none-of-these              | forcing a low-confidence answer     |

## Accessibility and inclusion hypotheses

- `HYPOTHESIS`: bilingual labels may support some English/Simplified-Chinese
  comparison needs.
- `NOT_YET_TESTED`: whether translations are equally familiar or useful across
  participant groups.
- `NOT_YET_TESTED`: whether color, motion, density, or specialist vocabulary
  creates avoidable access barriers.
- `NOT_YET_TESTED`: whether the interaction works under café noise, mobile
  attention constraints, or assistive technology.

Recruitment must not imply that participants require tasting expertise or that
their answers will be graded.
