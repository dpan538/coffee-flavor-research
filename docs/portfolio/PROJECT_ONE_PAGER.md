# Coffee Flavor Atlas — project one-pager

## Opportunity

Many people can perceive coffee differences but lack words to name, compare,
and remember them. Professional forms and package notes can add vocabulary while
also adding cognitive load or suggestion bias.

## Concept

Coffee Flavor Atlas investigates a short, adaptive path from preparation and
roast context to sensory questions and a small set of perception-compatible
references. It supports uncertainty and abstention. It does not claim to taste
the coffee, find one correct answer, or grade the user.

## Who it is for

Curious café customers, home brewers, beginners in specialty coffee, and people
comparing their impression with bean, menu, or package language.

## What exists now

- a responsive bilingual atlas, descriptor detail, comparison, and methodology
  interface;
- a validated PostgreSQL knowledge base with provenance, rights, context,
  duplicate, review, and gate contracts;
- deterministic retrieval and explicit unresolved behavior;
- professional evidence acquisition and descriptor-first audit receipts;
- user-study and ML evaluation plans; and
- generated public status and claim verification.

## What is not yet current

No first-party participant data, trained ranking model, embedding experiment,
deep-learning run, calibrated adaptive policy, installable PWA shell, or
production sensory probability exists.

## Research value

The project tested and rejected an acquisition strategy that confused source
scale with professional descriptor scale. It now separates professional,
industry, consumer, and first-party evidence; separates acquisition from review
and model eligibility; and requires row-level evidence for human review claims.

## Next evidence step

Run a separately authorized, consented usability pilot or continue
rights-cleared professional descriptor review. Model baselines follow only when
the relevant label, rights, and grouped-split gates are met.
